American Bullfrog

The American bullfrog (known in Latin as Lithobates catesbeianus – which is a scientific name that stands for its classification inside the Ranidae family, and “catesbeianus” comes from the last name of Mark Catesby, an English naturalist) is a species of amphibians of the order Anura (1). They are really big; their size varies between 10 and 20 centimeters from their snout to their cloaca (2). Their weight goes from 60 and 900 grams. Even though the larvae are huge compared to other frogs, the length of these babies can be between 15 and 18 centimeters (about twice the length of the long edge of a credit card). The dorsal color varies from a light green or olive-green to a greenish brown, it is common that they are seen green or brown spots throughout their backs. The head is wide and plain, it has a crease that starts behind both eyes and finishes around the eardrum. The bulky belly is whitish, and it has grey spots. The rear legs have something like bars or dark spots. All toes, except for the fourth one, expect interdigital membranes (3). In the male bullfrog the eardrum has a bigger diameter than the one the eye possesses, with a dark edge. However, in the female bullfrog the eardrum diameter has the same size than the diameter of the eye. These beasts come from the East of North America, but they have been little invaders in other zones of America, and even of Europe and Asia. In Mexico they are considered exotic animals, in regions such as Sonora Desert, central plateau, Baja California’s peninsula and Tamaulipas, among others. In United States of America, they are considered exotic animals in California and Colorado, where they appeared at the beginning of the XX century. A funny fact is that this species is included in the list of IUCN (International Union for Conservation of Nature) called “100 of the World’s Worst Invasive Species”.

Both as native species or invader species, it is usual that the bullfrog live in aquatic and semi-aquatic environments. Their natural habitats are masses of water, that can be understood as bodies of stagnant water that contain aquatic vegetation. Examples of where these huge frogs can be found include lakes, swamps, pools, water deposits, oasis, brims of rivers, irrigation trenches, and artificial lakes. they grow in different types of weather, from winter cold and summer heat places to subtropical (adjacent zone to the tropics) and Mediterranean (mild and rainy winters and hot and dry summers) and weather ecosystems. This animal dwells in the range of 0 to 1900 meters above the level of the sea, which is equal to 1.18 miles. The great expansion of the bullfrog across the globe is due the economic value this amphibian carries as food in countries like Mexico, among many others. This little bull is used in activities such as, for instance, hunting (sporting) -where obviously they are the target-. Also, as a biologic model in academic environments, or in scientific ones where the bullfrog is used in experiments allocated to reinforce the knowledge in the fields of reproductive physiology (4), neurobiology (5), ontology (6), and endocrinology (7). In spite of this, according to a report issued on 2019 by the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN), the American bullfrog is a low-level concern species, in respect of extinction.

Description

  • The American bullfrog holds the record of being the biggest frog in North America, they measure betwixt 100 and 175 millimeters (3.93 and 6.88 inches), but it is possible that they measure up to 460 mm (18.11 inches!).
  • They can weigh up to 1 kilogram (2.20 pounds).
  • Small vertebrates such as snakes, fishes, birds, little rodents, and other frogs and tadpoles compound the diet. It is rare but it has been registered that the bullfrog feeds on bats, worms, insects, and crustaceans, too!

Ecology

For their disgrace, they appear on other species’ menu, like many birds (for instance big herons), river otters (Lontra canadensis), predatory fishes, and occasionally other amphibians. Their arch enemies are kingfishers and alligators. Another funny fact is that these frogs can resist the viper’s poison.

Taxonomy

For their taxonomy, most scientific authorities accept the specific name Rana catesbaiana, this being the utilized name in publications since the year 2006. Nevertheless, recently it was discovered that this frog must be classified in the Lithobates family (8), which is a large family to which many distinct species belong, for example the wood frog.



This beast appears on the list of worrisome invaders exotic species issued by the European Union.
